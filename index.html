Senate Votes on War Powers, Firearms, and Cannabis

Public record of one senator set next to the citizen-level mapping approach for the same three priorities.

Scope

This page lists verifiable votes and positions by Senator Rand Paul (R-KY) on three topics: authorizations for the use of military force, federal firearms restrictions, and federal cannabis policy. It does not assess motives or overall alignment with any administration. It only places the record beside the question a voter using the mapping strategy would ask: which viable candidate in a given race is closer to a stated priority on the office that controls it.

War powers and AUMFs

  • Repeatedly offered or supported amendments and standalone measures to repeal or sunset the 2001 and 2002 AUMFs.
  • Voted for repeal of the 1991 and 2002 AUMFs when those measures reached the floor.
  • Forced recorded votes on repeal language even when the amendments were defeated by large margins.

Under the mapping approach, a voter who ranks “no new or open-ended military authorizations” highly would treat a senator’s votes on AUMF repeal and war-powers resolutions as directly relevant, regardless of party.

Federal firearms rules

  • Voted against the 2022 bipartisan gun legislation.
  • Voted to disapprove the ATF stabilizing-brace rule that expanded the definition of short-barreled rifles under the NFA.
  • Consistently opposed new federal statutory restrictions on common firearms and accessories.

A voter who ranks protection of existing firearms configurations highly would count these votes as aligned with that priority on the Senate office that helps set federal rules.

Cannabis

  • Supported measures to limit federal enforcement against state-legal cannabis activity and to ease research barriers.
  • Public positions have favored removing cannabis from Schedule I and respecting state regulatory regimes.

For a voter who ranks federal rescheduling or non-interference with state legalization highly, these positions are the relevant Senate-level signal.

How the citizen strategy uses this

The mapping method does not require electing any particular senator. It asks, in each race, which of the viable candidates is closer to the voter’s ranked priority on the power that office holds. A senator’s record on AUMFs is evidence about war-powers votes; a representative’s record on appropriations riders is evidence about agency enforcement; a state legislator’s record is evidence about state firearms or cannabis statutes. The same three priorities can therefore be advanced by different candidates in different races.
